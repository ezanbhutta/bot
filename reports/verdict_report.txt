H-B claimed newly listed tokens systematically decline after listing, so fading/avoiding the listing has positive expectancy. Descriptively the dump is real: 42% of listings trade below their first-hour price 14 days later, 75% peak within the first 24h, and the median max drawdown inside 14 days is 66%. The tested rule — short at +1h|14d — survived walk-forward, deflation, PBO, friction and the sample-size gate. READ THE VERDICT NARROWLY. It validates the fade SIGNAL as measured on spot prices; it does NOT authorize deploying capital. (1) On SPOT the only actionable form is: do not buy listings; there is nothing to short. (2) Short execution lives on futures, and this run does NOT model funding/borrow: fresh listings often trade with deeply negative perp funding (shorts PAY, at extremes ~0.1-1% per 8h), which over a 14d hold could consume the entire measured edge — per HYPOTHESIS.md the short side requires a separate futures-market validation with funding data before any deployment. (3) A perp must EXIST at entry time; the shortable universe is a subset of these events. (4) The return profile is short-squeeze-shaped (skew -3.1): most events win, rare events lose multiples — position sizing is the difference between this edge and ruin. Why it may persist: the fade side is capacity-limited and uncrowded — retail flow is structurally on the buy side at listings, and the borrow/funding cost that protects it from arbitrage is exactly the cost that must be validated next.
